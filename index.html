Master of Science · Data Science & Health

Babak Badakhshan, MSc

Babak is a quantitative urban health geographer and (spatial) data scientist who studies how urban built-natural-social environments shape people's health and health behaviours by combining novel spatial big data with state-of-the-art methodological solutions. His work contributes to fields of environmental epidemiology, geoinformatics, urban planning and design, and sustainable transportation planning. Babak's ultimate goal is to generate actionable insights for urban policymakers and agencies to co-create healthy, inclusive, and just living environments that support both people and the environment in a changing world.

About

I possess a multidisciplinary research background in sustainable urban mobility, geographical information science, and environmental health science. Through my research, I aim to understand the complex pathways through which our living surroundings, from the air we breathe to the noise we experience and the environment we see, shape health, mental well-being, and societal inequalities. The focal point of my work is using activity spaces to model and map individualised dynamic environmental exposure in daily movements to better support urban practitioners and public health agencies in building healthier, fairer and sustainable cities.

I completed my bachelor's degree in Geography and Urban Planning with a specialisation in applications of GIS and remote sensing for sustainable urban and regional planning. Then, I moved to Kharazmi University in Tehran, Iran, where I defended my master's thesis titled "linking multiple nature exposure types during daily urban travel to health and well-being: a mixed-method approach" with distinction. In my dissertation, I deeply investigated how urban natural environments shape people’s health and well-being by combining multiple nature exposure metrics (i.e., visibility, availability and accessibility) during daily travel routes, origin (individuals' home locations) and destinations (e.g., work and everyday errands) and the possibility of street-level greenery on healthy mobility behaviours.

After completing my master's degree, I took on several roles in different institutions, first as an urban planner and later as a (spatial) data scientist, working on various projects related to urban mobility and spatial accessibility modelling, urban resilience and environmental exposure, and human–place relationships. During this time, I also established my own small training academy ("Geoanalytica") to teach and consult in GIScience, data science, and remote sensing.

So far, I have successfully taught and mentored over a hundred GIS and AI enthusiasts from various disciplines. I actively contribute to the body of scholarly knowledge in the domain of urban planning and transportation through writing and publishing scientific manuscripts in highly reputable peer-reviewed journals.
